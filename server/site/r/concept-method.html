Kleos measures how you come across: pace, pauses, where your voice goes at the end of a sentence, where your eyes go. Every number comes from the recording and sits against a range, from published research or our own, and says which.

Measu

Half a minute

Strangers watched silent clips of teachers, some only seconds long. Their snap ratings matched the students’ after a full term. Ambady & Rosenthal, 1993.

30 s

Tone, not words

Surgeons’ voices with the words filtered out. The more dominant the tone, the more likely a malpractice claim. Ambady et al., 2002.

10 s

Counted, then rated

Across 138 mock interviews, what a computer could count, like fluency, fillers and smiling, predicted how people were rated. Naim et al., 2015.

What we never do

We don’t rate your face, your body, your accent or the natural sound of your voice. Only what you can train.

It isn’t a medical or psychological test, and it doesn’t diagnose anything.

The video is measured, then deleted, unless you’ve chosen to help train Poise.

What we’re doing next

A rater study: real strangers rating thirty-second takes, so every Kleos range is checked against what people actually hear. Until then, those ranges say they’re ours.
